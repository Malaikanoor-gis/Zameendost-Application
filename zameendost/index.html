How ZameenDost works · یہ کیسے کام کرتا ہے
  1. Weather from NASA. For every day since your last irrigation, NASA POWER supplies temperature, humidity, wind and sunshine for your field; the app turns them into how much water the air took (FAO-56 Penman-Monteith).
  2. Your crop from orbit. MODIS and VIIRS satellites measure how green the field is; greener crop means more water use, so the crop coefficient follows the real canopy, not a table.
  3. A water budget for the root zone. Water used by the crop minus rain, day by day. When the easily available water is gone, it is time to irrigate — unless useful rain is forecast within two days and the crop can wait for it.
  4. Checked against other satellites. Land surface temperature (a thirsty crop runs hot), SMAP soil moisture and GRACE water storage are shown beside the model so disagreement is visible.

Past weather: NASA POWER (MERRA-2 meteorology, CERES solar) → FAO-56 Penman-Monteith ET₀ computed in your browser. Forecast: Open-Meteo. Satellite: NASA GIBS — MODIS/VIIRS NDVI, MODIS land surface temperature, SMAP soil moisture, GPM IMERG — read pixel-by-pixel through NASA's published colormaps. Crop coefficients: FAO Irrigation & Drainage Paper 56. Place search: GeoNames (via Open-Meteo) and OpenStreetMap (via Photon); place names: BigDataCloud; map: Esri imagery with village names from OpenStreetMap; street map by OpenStreetMap France. A decision aid for any field in Pakistan, piloted in Khushab — not a substitute for looking at your crop and soil.
